Guide

How to spot a fake Warsaw listing

Searching from abroad, you can’t stand in the flat. These are the patterns to look out for, and what to do instead.

Team review of this list confirm

  1. Sign 1 A deposit before any viewing

    The landlord asks for a deposit or a reservation fee before anyone has seen the flat, often because many people are interested.

    What to do instead

    Pay nothing until you, or someone you trust, has been inside or watched a live video. A real landlord can wait for a viewing.

  2. Sign 2 No live video

    You ask for a live video call and get excuses, or the same photos again.

    What to do instead

    Ask for a live video in which the person walks through the flat and shows the view from a window. Photos and recorded videos can be copied from other ads.

  3. Sign 3 Pressure to decide today

    You’re told someone else will take it tonight unless you pay now.

    What to do instead

    Take the time to check. A flat worth having is still worth having after a viewing.

  4. Sign 4 A price far below the usual range

    A room or flat offered for much less than similar ones nearby.

    What to do instead

    Compare with the usual ranges: a room in a shared flat costs 1,400–2,200 PLN a month, a 1–2-room flat 2,800–4,500 PLN. If it looks too cheap, ask why before anything else.

  5. Sign 5 The conversation moves off the platform

    Soon after your first message, you’re asked to continue on another app or by private email, and to pay there.

    What to do instead

    Keep the conversation where the ad is until you’ve seen the flat, and never pay by a method you can’t trace.

Never send a deposit for a flat nobody has seen.

Source Price ranges: Kozminski University, student living costs (May 2026).
